Here are some answers to common questions asked. If there is something missing please
[contact us](/contact) or [raise an issue on GitHub.](https://github.com/ld-archive/ld-archive-web/issues)

## Do I have to have the rights to material I upload?

**Yes** this is essential and one of the terms of service of the Humanities Commons. 
You are responsible for complying with all the terms of that site.

## Do the things I upload need to be perfect?

No. Upload documents that show your process. That might be different versions of a plan, or digital copies of plans you have drawn on.

## What will happen to material I upload?

It will be freely available for the world to see on this site and [Humanities Commons](https://hcommons.org/groups/lighting-design-archive). 

## Can I upload something now and set it for release in the future.

Yes. Humanities Commons enables restricting and then placing an embargo on material you upload. 
Allowing you to archive today but make available in the future.

## Are pictures allowed?

Yes, anything you have the rights to. Where the photographer was not you, you are encouraged to get
the photographer's permission for archiving specifically. You could also just include additional
infromation about where to find the photographs for the project. 

## What meta data should I include?

Metadata is key in making your material discoverable in the future. Meta data can be edited after uploading files.

## Do I have to sign up to Humanities Commons?

Yes. In order to upload you must join Humanities Commons. Sign up is easy, and doesn't take long.

## What is an ORCID and should I get one?

[ORCID](http://orcid.org) is a unique identifier that connects researchers with their research. 
Research is used in the broadest possible sense. 
There aren't any criteria for getting an ORCID you just sign up.
Including an ORCID is enterly optional, however, if you have an ORCID all future files you upload 
can be identified as being your work. It's the easiest way to remove uncertenty between people with 
the same or similar names. 

Getting an ORCID is free, and lasts forever.
